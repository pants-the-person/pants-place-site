---
title: How I learned to stop worrying and love the bevel
date: 1998-10-24
kind: blog
summary: Every pixel should look like it can be pressed. That is the entire design philosophy.
tags:
  - design
  - windows
---

I have been thinking about why Windows 98 looks the way it does, and I think the answer is **bevels**.

Everything is a raised surface. Every button wants to be pressed. Every input field looks like it has been carved out of the window with a tiny chisel. The whole interface is a promise that if you click hard enough, something mechanical will happen.

Modern software is obsessed with flatness. Everything is a rectangle with a soft shadow, and nothing looks like it can be pushed. I hate it. Where is the texture? Where is the 2-pixel highlight?

I have been building my own little web desktop in HTML and CSS, and the rule is simple: if an element is interactive, it gets a bevel. Buttons are raised. Text fields are sunken. The taskbar is a small metal shelf at the bottom of the screen.

The hard part is the title bar. Navy on the left, cerulean on the right, white bold text, grey when the window is inactive. It has to be exactly right or the whole illusion collapses. I have spent two evenings on the gradient alone.

Worth it.

-- pants
